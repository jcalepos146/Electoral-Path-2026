Why does Grok’s estimate come out near D+6?

The quoted estimate scales J.L. Partners’ rounded 10-point turnout contribution by the ratio of the hypothetical turnout gap to the reported 13-point gap, then adds the rounded five-point switching contribution to the R+2 presidential baseline. At a four-point gap, this is −2 + 10 × (4 ÷ 13) + 5 = D+6.1.

At the currently selected gap: —. Proportionality between turnout-rate differences and vote-margin swings is an extra analyst assumption. It is not a counterfactual estimated by J.L. Partners. The calculations below are alternative models, not an uncertainty interval.

How this isolates the turnout gap

The scenario reverses the four-point 2022 difference, rather than copying the literal 71% and 67% levels into 2026. Let H and T be the sizes of the 2024 Harris and Trump coalitions. Solve H × Harris turnout + T × Trump turnout = the selected reference’s combined number of voters, and Harris turnout − Trump turnout = the selected gap. Rates are percentages of each group. Each state then follows the existing simulator’s turnout model, holding within-group vote choice fixed.

For the J.L. Partners option, calculate each state’s modeled margin under the selected rates minus its modeled margin at the 78% / 65% reference rates, then add that difference to its counted margin. Thus the reference itself reproduces the counted outcome. Within-group House voting still comes from the existing voter-only dataset, not an inferred Harris-voter breakdown from J.L. Partners.

Applying this scenario resets the other simulator controls, including any no-war adjustment. The seat totals are illustrative model outputs; uncalled races use their leaders and Alaska uses the existing ranked-choice approximation. This estimates a turnout-only change, not how much of the polling error turnout caused. The 2025 New Jersey polling comparison and the 2026 J.L. Partners decomposition provide different kinds of evidence.

Calculation and limits

Fit an unweighted least-squares line to approval against field-end date within the selected window. Only polls whose entire fieldwork starts inside the window and ends before the war cutoff enter the fit. Start the hypothetical path at the last prewar observation, then extend zero, half or all of the fitted monthly slope through Nov. 3; approval is bounded to 0–100%.

Hypothetical House margin = counted 2026 D−R margin − sensitivity coefficient × (hypothetical approval − observed October approval). Both approval readings concern all adults. Their difference is applied to the election margin; the voter-only approval slider is not substituted for either reading. Applying this scenario resets the simulator to its default approval and turnout before applying just this national shift, avoiding double counting.

The scenario holds turnout and candidate effects fixed. It does not assign all post-February changes to the war, and it can imply a worse Republican outcome for some trend choices. Uniform shifts across states and districts are illustrative, especially for ranked-choice contests and runoffs. Shifts beyond the simulator’s ±20-point range cannot be applied.

    Calculation, sources and limits

    For each 2024 cohort, returning voters = 2024 vote total × assumed 2028 return turnout. Allocate only those returners using conditional Democratic/Republican/other shares. Add a separate, disjoint number of voters who did not cast a 2024 presidential vote. Sum all votes and divide the D−R difference by all modeled votes, including other candidates. There is no automatic carryover from the 2026 House margin.

    This simplified national model does not explicitly estimate future eligibility, deaths or migration, candidate effects, state turnout, electoral votes or win probabilities. The analyst-selected return rates and entrant volume absorb unmodeled demographic changes. Pew’s historical panel estimates are benchmarks, not measurements of 2028.       How the simulator works

      Each race starts at its actual 2026 margin, then gets three adjustments: the national environment shift, the approval change, and the turnout changes for its state.

      Turnout: the 2026 Voter Poll split voters by their 2024 vote. Each group's turnout rate is estimated by dividing its share of the roughly 119 million 2026 House votes counted by Nov. 24 by its 2024 vote total; for people who skipped 2024, the denominator is the eligible population (UF Election Lab) minus 2024 ballots. Each state's electorate is estimated from its own 2024 or 2020 result, scaled so the nation matches the poll. A slider sets the turnout rate nationally, and every state's group moves by the same proportion. Each group then votes the way it did nationally in the poll. With the 2020 baseline, 2020 Biden and Trump voters are assumed to turn out in the same mix and vote like 2024 Harris and Trump voters.

      Approval: each point moves 1% of voters between "somewhat disapprove" and "somewhat approve", using those groups' House vote in the poll. This assumes only soft opinions change.

      House districts use each district's count as of Nov. 24; uncontested districts count as 100-point margins. Turnout effects use the state's electorate. Where an independent was the main challenger to a Republican, the independent is treated as the challenger. With a 50–50 Senate the vice president, a Republican, breaks ties. This is a what-if tool, not a forecast.

      Voter trends: the national House exit-poll figures transcribed from Wikipedia’s 2026 House elections page, labeled there as Edison. These are survey estimates, not counts. The separately labeled J.L. Partners survey includes nonvoters and uses a different denominator; AP-NORC’s approval series covers all adults.

      County map: 2020 and 2024 presidential results by county from the tonmcg/US_County_Level_Election_Results_08-24 dataset; 2026 county results for Senate, governor and House races from civicAPI, unofficial as of Nov. 24. civicAPI doesn't publish where its data comes from; its statewide totals agree with AP-reported results within 1.5 points everywhere, and closer where counting has finished. County outlines from the us-atlas package.

      Ballot measures: counts from civicAPI; descriptions from Wikipedia's 2026 ballot measures page where the measure could be matched. Turnout: votes in each state's largest 2026 statewide count compared with presidential votes summed from county results.

      Simulator: starting margins from civicAPI counts as of Nov. 24 (race calls stay with AP); turnout rates estimated from the 2026 Voter Poll, the roughly 119 million House votes counted by Nov. 24, 2024 and 2020 presidential totals, and the UF Election Lab's 2024 eligible-population and ballots estimates.

      Not included: precinct results, state-by-state demographic estimates, and live updates. This is a fixed snapshot and does not refresh on its own.
