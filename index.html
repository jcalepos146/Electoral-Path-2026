Results by county

Every county with a 2026 count, unofficial as of Nov. 9. "Top race" uses each state's Senate race, else its governor race, else its House races, skipping races where an independent was a top-two finisher. Alaska has no 2024 or 2020 results by borough, so it shows 2026 margins only; Connecticut's 2024 results are by planning region, so its 2024 comparisons are blank.

Swing: the county's 2026 margin minus its presidential margin. Trend: that swing minus the national shift over the same period (— toward Democrats since 2024, — since 2020). Blue counties moved toward Democrats more than the nation did; red counties moved less, or toward Republicans. Candidates matter: a strong or weak nominee shows up as swing and trend. House layers leave out counties in a district one party didn't contest.

How the simulator works

Each race starts at its actual 2026 margin, then gets three adjustments: the national environment shift, the approval change, and the turnout changes for its state.

Turnout: the 2026 Voter Poll split voters by their 2024 vote. Each group's turnout rate is estimated by dividing its share of the roughly 115 million 2026 House votes counted by Nov. 9 by its 2024 vote total; for people who skipped 2024, the denominator is the eligible population (UF Election Lab) minus 2024 ballots. Each state's electorate is estimated from its own 2024 or 2020 result, scaled so the nation matches the poll. A slider sets the turnout rate nationally, and every state's group moves by the same proportion. Each group then votes the way it did nationally in the poll. With the 2020 baseline, 2020 Biden and Trump voters are assumed to turn out in the same mix and vote like 2024 Harris and Trump voters.

Approval: each point moves 1% of voters between "somewhat disapprove" and "somewhat approve", using those groups' House vote in the poll. This assumes only soft opinions change.

House districts use each district's count as of Nov. 9; uncontested districts count as 100-point margins. Turnout effects use the state's electorate. Where an independent was the main challenger to a Republican, the independent is treated as the challenger. With a 50–50 Senate the vice president, a Republican, breaks ties. This is a what-if tool, not a forecast.

Sources and method

Race results and calls: Associated Press tallies as compiled on Wikipedia's 2026 House, Senate and governor election pages (snapshot Nov. 8, 2026; calls rechecked Nov. 9). Vote shares in the race tables come from those pages and may lag the latest count.

Notes on uncalled races come from AP reporting on the Mississippi count and on Alaska's ranked-choice timetable. "Changed party" compares the winner with the party that held the seat or office before the election; for House seats redrawn mid-decade, it follows the source's gain and hold labels.

Voter trends: the 2026 Voter Poll conducted by SSRS for the AP and partner networks, national House results as published by CNN and transcribed on Wikipedia. These are survey estimates with a margin of error, not counts.

County map: 2020 and 2024 presidential results by county from the tonmcg/US_County_Level_Election_Results_08-24 dataset; 2026 county results for Senate, governor and House races from civicAPI, unofficial as of Nov. 9. civicAPI doesn't publish where its data comes from; its statewide totals agree with AP-reported results within 1.5 points everywhere, and closer where counting has finished. County outlines from the us-atlas package.

Ballot measures: counts from civicAPI; descriptions from Wikipedia's 2026 ballot measures page where the measure could be matched. Turnout: votes in each state's largest 2026 statewide count compared with presidential votes summed from county results.

Simulator: starting margins from civicAPI counts as of Nov. 9 (race calls stay with AP); turnout rates estimated from the 2026 Voter Poll, the roughly 115 million House votes counted by Nov. 9, 2024 and 2020 presidential totals, and the UF Election Lab's 2024 eligible-population and ballots estimates.

Not included: precinct results, state-by-state demographic estimates, and live updates. This is a fixed snapshot and does not refresh on its own.
